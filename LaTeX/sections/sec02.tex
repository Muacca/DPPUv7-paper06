\section{Geometry and variation domain}

Use an oriented orthonormal coframe. The real symmetric spatial structure matrix $n$ is time independent and can be diagonalized by a constant $SO(3)$ frame rotation. Spatial indices run over $1,2,3$ and spacetime indices over $0,1,2,3$. Repeated spatial indices in the structure equation are summed.

\stepcounter{equation}
\begin{equation}\tag{G-01}\label{eq:G-01}
\begin{aligned}
g_{ab}&=\mathrm{diag}(-1,1,1,1),\qquad \epsilon_{0123}=+1,\quad \epsilon^{0123}=-1,\\
e^0&=Ndt,\qquad e^i=q\sigma^i,\qquad
d\sigma^i=\tfrac12 n_{ij}\epsilon_{jkl}\sigma^k\wedge\sigma^l,\\
D&=N^{-1}d/dt,\qquad H=Dq/q,\qquad
\mathcal V_{\Sigma}=\int_{\Sigma}\sigma^{123},\quad q,N>0.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}

This class-A representation satisfies the Jacobi identity. In a diagonal frame let $n_i$ be the eigenvalues and $k_i$ the sectional-curvature coefficient in the complementary spatial plane. The $i$th spatial Ricci eigenvalue is the sum of the other two $k_i$. The scalar-curvature parameter is defined by the resulting curvature.

\stepcounter{equation}
\begin{equation}\tag{G-02}\label{eq:G-02}
\begin{aligned}
\ell_i&=\tfrac12(n_1+n_2+n_3)-n_i,\qquad
k_i=n_i\ell_i-\ell_j\ell_k,\\
{}^{(3)}R&=\frac{2(k_1+k_2+k_3)}{q^2}=\frac{6\chi}{q^2},\\
6\chi&=\tfrac12(\mathrm{tr}\,n)^2-\mathrm{tr}(n^2),\qquad
{}^{(3)}\mathrm{Ric}_{ii}=\frac{k_j+k_k}{q^2}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}

In (G-02), $(i,j,k)$ denotes each cyclic triple, with no further summation. The four reference geometries are fixed below. The spherical $q$ is the physical round radius, as in Paper05.

\begin{center}\small

On the torus all three angular coordinates have period $2\pi$. Spatial coordinates are written $x^1,x^2,x^3$. For Nil take the coframe $dx^1,dx^2,dx^3-x^1dx^2$ and the lattice generators $(x^1,x^2,x^3)\mapsto(x^1+2\pi,x^2,x^3+2\pi x^2)$, $(x^1,x^2,x^3)\mapsto(x^1,x^2+2\pi,x^3)$ and $(x^1,x^2,x^3)\mapsto(x^1,x^2,x^3+4\pi^2)$. Each identification preserves the coframe.

For Sol use $dx^1,e^{x^1}dx^2,e^{-x^1}dx^3$. Let $A=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}2&1\\1&1\end{smallmatrix}\right)$, with eigenvalues $\lambda=(3+\sqrt5)/2$ and $\lambda^{-1}$. Identify a shift of $x^1$ by $\log\lambda$ with the fiber map $\mathrm{diag}(\lambda^{-1},\lambda)$, using the area-one integer lattice transported to eigen-coordinates. The coframe descends to this mapping torus. Before diagonalization, $n_{23}=n_{32}=1$ are its only nonzero entries. This is class A, type VI$_0$ \cite{paper4}.

Define geometric torsion by the first Cartan equation and fix its two real amplitudes by the following ansatz. Again, the spatial wedge is displayed once for each cyclic triple.

\stepcounter{equation}
\begin{equation}\tag{G-03}\label{eq:G-03}
T^a=de^a+\omega^a{}_{b}\wedge e^b,\qquad
T^0=0,\qquad T^i=\frac V3e^{0i}+\frac{2\eta}{q}e^{jk}.
\end{equation}

\stepcounter{equation}
\begin{equation}\tag{G-04}\label{eq:G-04}
\begin{aligned}
a_i&=(\ell_i-\eta)/q,\qquad b=H-V/3,\\
\omega^0{}_{i}&=\omega^i{}_{0}=b e^i,\qquad
\omega^j{}_{k}=a_i e^i,\qquad \omega^k{}_{j}=-a_i e^i.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}

Substitution of (G-04) into (G-03) reproduces the specified torsion. Profiles are sufficiently smooth, while the spatial shape and quotient lattice are fixed. Formulas for a general structure matrix are local identities; the explicit spatial integrals use the fixed compact quotients above. Section 5 tests how equations obtained after this restriction relate to the full independent connection and coframe equations.

\subsection{Distinct variation domains}

The common-scale, two-amplitude family uses fixed real symmetric $n$ and $q,N,\eta,V$; this is the domain of the time-dependent curvature formulas in Section 6. For shape, we first solve the independent connection equation and use the LC action with $e^i=q(e^{\mathsf B})^i{}_j\sigma^j$, $\mathsf B^T=\mathsf B$, $\mathrm{tr}\mathsf B=0$. The five-component matrix $\mathsf B$ differs from the endpoint generator $B_{\mathrm{end}}$. The physical structure matrix is $m=q^{-1}e^{\mathsf B}ne^{\mathsf B}$.

A positive definite constant coefficient metric in the fixed global coframe descends to the same quotient. A constant congruence need not preserve the lattice and is not identified with a coordinate gauge transformation. The rotated coframe in Section 7 and Appendix A compares representations of the same static flat torus metric; it does not identify the entire dynamical constant-shape-coefficient families of the two frames.
