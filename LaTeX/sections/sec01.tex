\section{Introduction}

Homogeneous reduction of Lorentzian Einstein--Cartan gravity involves several related objects: local curvature contractions, spatial integrals, trajectories of the reduced action, and solutions of the full field equations. We compare the fixed reference geometries $T^3$, Nil, Sol and $S^3$, asking which results are controlled by their scalar-curvature parameter $\chi$. The model has no curvature-square term, matter source or cosmological constant, and its complete Nieh--Yan (NY) coupling is constant \cite{Hehl1976,NiehYan1982}.

The common-scale family consists of a fixed spatial metric with a constant class-A structure matrix, one time-dependent scale and two geometric torsion amplitudes. After eliminating torsion, we also vary all five homogeneous tracefree shape components. Nil and Sol have anisotropic spatial curvature. A common scale multiplies all spatial directions by the same factor; it does not make their metrics isotropic. The quantity $\chi$ describes a normalized reference metric, not a general topological invariant.

Earlier papers study reduced potentials, curvature identities, homogeneous deformations and torsion variations \cite{paper1,paper2,paper3,paper3ec}. Paper04 treats structure matrices, quadratic forms and boundary data on four fixed compact quotients \cite{paper4}; Paper05 develops the Lorentzian action, endpoint terms and homogeneous variations on the sphere normalized by its physical radius \cite{paper5}. Here we derive curvature formulas for a constant class-A structure matrix and compare the four reduced orbit problems with the full field equations.

\subsection{Main results}

After separating complete NY into an endpoint term, both torsion amplitudes are auxiliary and vanish uniquely. The remaining lapse constraint classifies real reduced trajectories by the sign of $\chi$: the spherical constraint surface is empty, the torus has a positive constant scale, and Nil and Sol have two linear proper-time trajectories. The latter retain a nonzero spatial tracefree Einstein residual and therefore are not full source-free vacuum solutions.

We derive NY, Pontryagin, a covariant internal-dual contraction and a relative Pontryagin primitive for arbitrary time profiles. Within this family their local scalar expressions depend on the structure matrix through $\chi$, and the static mixed-torsion coefficient is $C=-9\chi$. Absolute Chern--Simons (CS) primitives, spatial tracefree Ricci curvature and the volume normalization of integrals describe the geometric information needed in addition to scalar curvature.

\subsection{Scope of the comparison}

Two questions organize the comparison: the validity and shape extension of the common-scale reduction, and the curvature information retained by $\chi,J_2,J_3$. On the torus, pure scale has a second-order obstruction, whereas each direction of a mixed scale--shear cone is realized by an exact vacuum family. For Nil and Sol, the residual determines the initial shape acceleration and the quartic scale coefficient.

The curvature calculation connects to left-invariant metric geometry \cite{Milnor1976}; the exact family uses the Kasner relations \cite{Kasner1921}. Integrability of linearized Einstein solutions has a broader history: the closed flat torus is the standard example of linearization instability \cite{BrillDeser1973}, and a vacuum spacetime with a compact constant-mean-curvature Cauchy surface and Killing fields is a conical point of the solution space \cite{FischerMarsdenMoncrief1980,ArmsMarsdenMoncrief1982}. Here necessity and sufficiency are proved directly in a finite homogeneous sector on a fixed lattice, without invoking a general linearization-stability theorem. Section 5 and Appendix D treat shape; Sections 6--7 and Appendix B treat invariants and the inverse problem.
