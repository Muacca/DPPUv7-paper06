\section{Bulk action and signed boundary completion}

Use the curvature of the same connection and include complete NY as an exterior derivative. The coupling $\theta_{\mathrm{NY}}$ is constant and $\kappa^2>0$. The boundaries are spacelike endpoint slices; the spatial quotient is closed.

\stepcounter{equation}
\begin{equation}\tag{B-01}\label{eq:B-01}
\begin{aligned}
S&=\int_M e^{0123}\frac{R_{\mathrm{EC}}}{2\kappa^2}
+\theta_{\mathrm{NY}}\int_M dQ+S_{\mathrm{GHY}},\\
R^a{}_{b}&=d\omega^a{}_{b}+\omega^a{}_{c}\wedge\omega^c{}_{b},\qquad Q=e^a\wedge T_a,\\
dQ&=T^a\wedge T_a-e^a\wedge e^b\wedge R_{ab}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}

Differentiating the time-dependent connection gives the following scalar-curvature decomposition. The derivative of the vector amplitude and its product with $H$ form one divergence after multiplication by the volume density.

\stepcounter{equation}
\begin{equation}\tag{B-02}\label{eq:B-02}
\begin{aligned}
R_{\mathrm{LC}}&=6(DH+2H^2+\chi/q^2),\\
R_{\mathrm{EC}}-R_{\mathrm{LC}}&=-6\eta^2/q^2+2V^2/3-2DV-6HV.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}

\stepcounter{equation}
\begin{equation}\tag{B-03}\label{eq:B-03}
\begin{aligned}
L_{\mathrm{raw}}&=L_0+\frac d{dt}(B_{\mathrm{EH}}+B_{\mathrm{EC}}+B_{\mathrm{NY}}),\\
L_0&=A_{\Sigma}q\left(NF-\frac{\dot q^2}{N}\right),\qquad
A_{\Sigma}=\frac{3\mathcal V_{\Sigma}}{\kappa^2},\\
F&=\chi-\eta^2+\frac{q^2V^2}{9}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}

\stepcounter{equation}
\begin{equation}\tag{B-04}\label{eq:B-04}
B_{\mathrm{EH}}=\frac{3\mathcal V_{\Sigma}q^2\dot q}{\kappa^2N},\qquad
B_{\mathrm{EC}}=-\frac{\mathcal V_{\Sigma}q^3V}{\kappa^2},\qquad
B_{\mathrm{NY}}=6\theta_{\mathrm{NY}}\mathcal V_{\Sigma}q^2\eta.
\end{equation}

Equation (B-03) is common to the four geometries. The structure matrix enters $F$ through $\chi$, while the quotient volume enters $A_{\Sigma}$. Complete NY is contained in $B_{\mathrm{NY}}$, so the bulk auxiliary equations and reduced trajectories are independent of its constant coupling.

The outward unit normal points to the future at the final slice and to the past at the initial slice. Use the LC extrinsic curvature and the signature factor in the GHY term \cite{GH1977,HH1996}.

\stepcounter{equation}
\begin{equation}\tag{B-05}\label{eq:B-05}
\begin{aligned}
\hat n_f&=+N^{-1}\partial_t,\quad \hat n_i=-N^{-1}\partial_t,\quad
\epsilon_n=\hat n^a\hat n_a=-1,\quad K_f=3H_f,\quad K_i=-3H_i,\\
S_{\mathrm{GHY}}&=\frac1{\kappa^2}\sum_{s=i,f}\epsilon_n\int_{\Sigma_s}\sqrt h K_s
=-[B_{\mathrm{EH}}]_i^f.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}

\stepcounter{equation}
\begin{equation}\tag{B-06}\label{eq:B-06}
S=\int_{t_i}^{t_f}L_0dt+[B_{\mathrm{EC}}+B_{\mathrm{NY}}]_i^f,\qquad
\delta q=\delta\eta=\delta V=0\quad\text{at both endpoints}.
\end{equation}

These fixed endpoint data suffice for the completed variational problem; neither the endpoint lapse nor the scale velocity needs to be fixed. If $L_0$ is chosen as the canonical representative, restoring the derivative of $B=B_{\mathrm{EC}}+B_{\mathrm{NY}}$ shifts the momenta as follows, without changing the bulk equations.

\stepcounter{equation}
\begin{equation}\tag{B-07}\label{eq:B-07}
\begin{aligned}
\Delta p_q&=-\frac{3\mathcal V_{\Sigma}q^2V}{\kappa^2}
+12\theta_{\mathrm{NY}}\mathcal V_{\Sigma}q\eta,\\
\Delta p_{\eta}&=6\theta_{\mathrm{NY}}\mathcal V_{\Sigma}q^2,\qquad
\Delta p_V=-\frac{\mathcal V_{\Sigma}q^3}{\kappa^2},\qquad \Delta p_N=0.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}

Prescribing nonzero endpoint torsion is distinct from the existence of a smooth stationary curve with those endpoint values. The auxiliary equations in Section 4 impose $\eta=V=0$ throughout the interval and, by continuity, at its endpoints. Boundary sources or discontinuous layers define different problems and are not included here.

\subsection{Canonical correspondence of endpoint representatives}

After GHY completion, write $B_{\mathrm{end}}=B_{\mathrm{EC}}+B_{\mathrm{NY}}$. Adding $dB_{\mathrm{end}}/dt$ gives $P_i=p_i+\partial_iB_{\mathrm{end}}$, including the auxiliary momenta. In $\widetilde p_i=P_i-\partial_iB_{\mathrm{end}}$, the constraints retain their original form. Appendix C gives the two-form and second-class constraints. Canonical equivalence refers to corresponding endpoint data: fixing the old and new momenta independently would generally define different boundary conditions.
