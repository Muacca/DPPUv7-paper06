\section{Common-scale reduction and shape evolution}

Complete NY with constant coupling does not change the bulk connection equation. Independent variation of the metric-compatible connection in the EC action, with no curvature-square term or spin source, gives the Cartan equation below. For a nondegenerate coframe it sets all torsion to zero. Appendix C proves this without imposing the two-amplitude ansatz.

\stepcounter{equation}
\begin{equation}\tag{E-01}\label{eq:E-01}
\epsilon_{abcd}\,e^c\wedge T^d=0
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad T^a=0.
\end{equation}

After torsion is eliminated, the full coframe equation is the vacuum Einstein equation. On the common-scale metric it splits as follows. The matrix $\Xi$ is the spatial tracefree Ricci tensor and is determined by the structure matrix.

\stepcounter{equation}
\begin{equation}\tag{E-02}\label{eq:E-02}
\begin{aligned}
G_{00}&=3(H^2+\chi/q^2),\qquad G_{0i}=0,\\
G_{ij}&=(-2DH-3H^2-\chi/q^2)\delta_{ij}+\Xi_{ij},\\
\Xi_{ij}&={}^{(3)}\mathrm{Ric}_{ij}-\frac{2\chi}{q^2}\delta_{ij}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}

Imposing (H-04) removes $G_{00}$ and the spatial trace, but leaves $\Xi$. In diagonal frames the four residuals are

\stepcounter{equation}
\begin{equation}\tag{E-03}\label{eq:E-03}
q^2\Xi_{T^3}=q^2\Xi_{S^3}=0,\qquad
q^2\Xi_{\mathrm{Nil}}=\tfrac13\mathrm{diag}(-1,-1,2),\qquad
q^2\Xi_{\mathrm{Sol}}=\tfrac13\mathrm{diag}(-4,2,2).
\end{equation}

Consequently the constant-scale torus satisfies the full vacuum equations, whereas the sphere has no real trajectory even in the reduction. The linear Nil and Sol trajectories are stationary curves of the fixed-shape variational problem, not full vacuum solutions. This does not exclude vacuum solutions with time-dependent anisotropic shapes on Nil or Sol.

For this action, the independent connection equation fixes all torsion, and the full coframe equation supplies the spatial-curvature condition remaining on a reduced trajectory. Algebraic auxiliary elimination, existence of real constraint data and satisfaction of the full field equations can thus be examined in sequence.

\subsection{Condition for a common-scale vacuum family}

\stepcounter{equation}
\begin{equation}\tag{E-04}\label{eq:E-04}
\begin{aligned}
f(n)&=\tfrac12(\mathrm{tr}n)^2-\mathrm{tr}(n^2)=6\chi,\\
X(n)&=q^2\Xi=2n^2-(\mathrm{tr}n)n+4\chi I,\qquad \mathrm{tr}X=0,\\
\delta L_{\mathrm{shape}}&=-\frac{N\mathcal V_{\Sigma}q}{\kappa^2}
 \mathrm{tr}(X\delta\mathsf B).
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}

\stepcounter{equation}
\begin{equation}\tag{E-05}\label{eq:E-05}
X=0\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad n\sim\mathrm{diag}(a,a,a)\ \text{or}\ \mathrm{diag}(a,a,0),\qquad a\in\mathbb R.
\end{equation}

Here $\sim$ denotes constant $SO(3)$ conjugacy. The first branch has $\chi=a^2/4$, so nonzero $a$ admits no real common-scale vacuum trajectory. The second is spatially flat with $\chi=0$ and gives static real vacuum families. This is a local classification for real symmetric $n$, not an existence assertion for new compact quotients. Appendix D proves it by five-direction variation and eigenvalue differences.

\subsection{Mixed constraint cone of the flat torus}

On the fixed-lattice $n=0$ torus, set $q=q_0e^{\epsilon\rho}$, $\mathsf B=\epsilon\mathsf B_1$, $N=N_0+\epsilon\delta N$ and $\tau=\int N_0dt$. The quadratic action, its linear solutions and the second-order coefficient of the original lapse equation give

\stepcounter{equation}
\begin{equation}\tag{S-01}\label{eq:S-01}
\begin{aligned}
L^{[2]}&=\frac{A_{\Sigma}q_0^3}{6N_0}\left[\mathrm{tr}(\dot{\mathsf B}_1^2)-6\dot\rho^2\right],\\
\rho&=\rho_0+h\tau,\qquad \mathsf B_1=\mathsf B_0+\Sigma\tau,\\
\Sigma^T&=\Sigma,\quad\mathrm{tr}\Sigma=0,\qquad \mathrm{tr}(\Sigma^2)=6h^2.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}

If $\Sigma=0$, then $h=0$, in agreement with (H-06) through $u=q_0\rho$. Nonzero mixed directions are integrable. For each direction with $h\ne0$, define the following exact metric on the fixed lattice; $\psi^{2\mathsf P}=\exp(2\mathsf P\log\psi)$ is positive definite.

\stepcounter{equation}
\begin{equation}\tag{S-02}\label{eq:S-02}
\begin{aligned}
\mathsf P&=\tfrac13 I+\frac{\Sigma}{3h},\quad M_0=\rho_0I+\mathsf B_0,\quad \psi=1+3\epsilon h\tau>0,\\
g_{\epsilon}(\tau)&=q_0^2e^{\epsilon M_0}\psi^{2\mathsf P}e^{\epsilon M_0},\\
\mathrm{tr}\mathsf P&=\mathrm{tr}(\mathsf P^2)=1,\qquad
q_{\epsilon}=q_0e^{\epsilon\rho_0}\psi^{1/3}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}

This is a Kasner-type vacuum family \cite{Kasner1921}, with tangent $\rho,\mathsf B_1$ at $\epsilon=0$. On any bounded time interval, $\psi>0$ holds uniformly for sufficiently small $\epsilon$. When $h=0$, the cone forces $\Sigma=0$ and static positive definite metric moduli realize the direction. In this homogeneous sector, the cone is thus necessary and sufficient for local integrability of each first-order tangent. This is the homogeneous form of the linearization instability of the flat torus \cite{BrillDeser1973}: the cone is the second-order condition attached to the static time translation, and the momentum constraints vanish identically for $n=0$ (Appendix D). Fixing the metric at both endpoints instead sets all affine first variations to zero. Appendix D includes noncommuting initial shape.

\subsection{Initial shape evolution on Nil and Sol}

In the diagonal LC vacuum sector choose $q=q_0$, $\beta_i=D\beta_i=0$ and $H_0^2=-\chi/q_0^2$ initially, with $e^i=qe^{\beta_i}\sigma^i$, $\sum_i\beta_i=0$. Set $\hat\tau=\tau/q_0$ and $h_{\ast}=H_0q_0$. The initial $X_i$ and $J_2=\mathrm{tr}(X^2)$ determine the exact Taylor coefficients

\stepcounter{equation}
\begin{equation}\tag{S-03}\label{eq:S-03}
\begin{aligned}
\beta_i&=-\tfrac12X_i\hat\tau^2+\tfrac56h_{\ast}X_i\hat\tau^3+O(\hat\tau^4),\\
q/q_0&=1+h_{\ast}\hat\tau-\tfrac1{36}J_2\hat\tau^4+O(\hat\tau^5).
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
