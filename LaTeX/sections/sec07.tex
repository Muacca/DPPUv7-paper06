\section{Curvature information and geometric representations}

Equations (B-03), (D-02) and (D-05) organize the reduced bulk action, local NY, Pontryagin and internal-dual scalars, and the relative primitive per reference volume by $\chi$. The curvature tensor itself retains the three separate $k_i$. Scalar-curvature control therefore does not identify the geometries or their full field equations.

For an explicit equal-$\chi$ comparison, take Nil with $n=\mathrm{diag}(0,0,-1)$ and a Sol-type reference with $n=\mathrm{diag}(0,1/2,-1/2)$. The latter uses a different reference normalization from the canonical Sol row. Both have $\chi=-1/12$, but

\stepcounter{equation}
\begin{equation}\tag{U-01}\label{eq:U-01}
\begin{aligned}
q^2\Xi_{\mathrm{Nil}}&=\mathrm{diag}(-1/3,-1/3,2/3),&\Phi_{0,\mathrm{Nil}}&=-1,\\
q^2\Xi_{\mathrm{Sol}/2}&=\mathrm{diag}(-1/3,1/6,1/6),&\Phi_{0,\mathrm{Sol}/2}&=0.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}

This is a comparison of local structure matrices, not an identification of quotient volumes or lattices. Integrated actions and endpoint quantities always carry $\mathcal V_{\Sigma}$. The sign of $\chi$ distinguishes reduced trajectories; it does not establish topology selection by comparing absolute action values.

The connection with the compact geometries of Paper04 is expressed through structure matrices and closed one-forms. Their ranks are $0,1,2,3$, and the dimensions of homogeneous closed one-forms are $3,2,1,0$ for $T^3$, Nil, Sol and $S^3$. Paper04 treats harmonic one-forms and quadratic forms on the specified compact quotients. These are geometric data characterizing the fixed coframes used here.

Bianchi class is determined by the trace of the structure constants. Appendix A evaluates the Jacobi condition and trace in a five-parameter representation and identifies its class-A sector, including Sol.

\subsection{Restricted \texorpdfstring{$\chi$}{chi}-universality and the information hierarchy}

Here restricted $\chi$-universality means precisely that, in the specified common-scale, two-amplitude family, the bulk action, local NY, Pontryagin and internal-dual scalars, and relative primitive per reference volume depend on $n$ through $\chi$. It does not assert universality of arbitrary curvature contractions or shape equations.

The equal-$\chi$ examples in (U-01) have $J_2=2/3$ and $1/6$. The following pair has equal $\chi,J_2$ but distinct $J_3$. It compares local off-shell curvatures at positive $\chi$, not real common-scale vacuum solutions.

At the same time variables, first minus second gives $\Delta C_{\mathrm{Ric}}=-12/q^6$, $\Delta C_R=96/q^6$. The tracefree Ricci spectrum is fixed by the polynomial below; adding $2\chi$ to each root gives the unit-scale spatial Ricci spectrum.

\stepcounter{equation}
\begin{equation}\tag{I-05}\label{eq:I-05}
\det(\lambda I-X)=\lambda^3-\tfrac12J_2\lambda-\tfrac13J_3,\qquad J_2\ge0,\quad \tfrac12J_2^3-3J_3^2\ge0.
\end{equation}

This reconstructs the Ricci spectrum at a point. For the inverse problem for $n$, three nonzero Ricci eigenvalues are realizable precisely when their product is positive; the solution is unique up to conjugacy and overall sign. Exactly one nonzero eigenvalue allows continuous nonuniqueness; exactly two is impossible. Zero Ricci gives the flat branch. Appendix B proves these statements. Nonunique structure matrices are not asserted to define locally nonisometric metrics.

\subsection{Frames on the same flat torus}

On the same $2\pi$-periodic $T^3$, rotate the coordinate coframe through the angle $\nu x^3$, $\nu\in\mathbb Z$. The resulting $n=(-\nu,-\nu,0)$ represents the same static flat metric as $n=0$. Its connection is pure gauge and its vector holonomy is the identity around every period. Appendix A gives the coframe and period calculation.

The number of closed forms within a fixed constant-coefficient coframe sector must therefore be distinguished from de Rham cohomology. This clarifies representation dependence while retaining the results for the specified coframes and quotients of Paper04. No spin-holonomy statement is made.
