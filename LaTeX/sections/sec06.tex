\section{Time-dependent curvature and relative endpoint quantities}

Compute the curvature of the same connection (G-04) before imposing static restrictions. The three spatial planes have distinct $y_i$, while the other blocks $x,z,w$ are common.

\stepcounter{equation}
\begin{equation}\tag{D-01}\label{eq:D-01}
\begin{aligned}
R^0{}_{i}&=x e^{0i}+w e^{jk},\qquad R^j{}_{k}=z e^{0i}+y_i e^{jk},\\
x&=Db+Hb,\qquad z=-D\eta/q,\qquad w=2b\eta/q,\\
y_i&=b^2+(k_i-\eta^2)/q^2,\qquad
\bar y=\tfrac13\sum_i y_i=b^2+(\chi-\eta^2)/q^2.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}

Define $Q$, complete NY and Pontryagin as oriented forms. In the internal-dual contraction $\mathcal B$, the form indices are raised with the Lorentz metric. With all internal indices summed, direct wedge products and component contractions give

\stepcounter{equation}
\begin{equation}\tag{D-02}\label{eq:D-02}
\begin{aligned}
Q&=\frac{6\eta}{q}e^{123},\qquad Q_{\Sigma}=6\mathcal V_{\Sigma}q^2\eta,\\
dQ&=\mathcal N e^{0123},\qquad \mathcal N=6(D\eta+2H\eta)/q,\\
\sum_{a,b}R_{ab}\wedge R^{ab}&=\mathcal P e^{0123},\qquad
\mathcal P=12(\bar y z-xw),\\
\mathcal B&=\tfrac1{16}\epsilon^{abef}R_{abcd}R_{ef}{}^{cd}
=3(w\bar y-xz).
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}

Summing the spatial-plane contributions gives $\sum_i y_i=3\bar y$, so the structure dependence of these scalar expressions reduces to $\chi$. This identity uses a constant class-A structure matrix, a common scale and the two-amplitude torsion ansatz. It is not an identity for arbitrary fields with time-dependent shape or tensor torsion. The duals in $\mathcal B$ and $\mathcal P$ act on different index pairs, and the two quantities are distinct.

\begin{center}\small
\begin{tabularx}{\textwidth}{@{}llX@{}}
\toprule
Family & Local quantities & Endpoint quantity\\\midrule
Static & $\mathcal N=\mathcal P=0$ & $Q_{\Sigma}$ can be nonzero\\
Dynamic AX & $\mathcal N,\mathcal P,\mathcal B$ need not vanish & $Q_{\Sigma}$ can be nonzero\\
Dynamic VT & $\mathcal N=\mathcal P=\mathcal B=0$ & $Q_{\Sigma}=0$\\
Dynamic MX & Given by (D-02) & $Q_{\Sigma}$ can be nonzero\\
Smooth auxiliary shell & $\mathcal N=\mathcal P=\mathcal B=0$ & $Q_{\Sigma}=0$\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}
\end{center}

Static means that all time derivatives of $q,\eta,V$ vanish. For dynamic VT, $\eta$ vanishes identically throughout the interval, so $D\eta=0$ as well. The auxiliary shell likewise imposes $\eta=V=0$ identically. A profile whose amplitudes vanish at only one instant is not the same restriction.

\stepcounter{equation}
\begin{equation}\tag{D-03}\label{eq:D-03}
\left.\mathcal B\right|_{\mathrm{static}}
=\frac{2V\eta}{9q^3}(-V^2q^2+9\eta^2+C),\qquad C=-9\chi.
\end{equation}

Equation (D-03) is the static limit of the covariant internal-dual contraction. Static AX and VT give zero, while static MX can be nonzero. Extracting the $V\eta$ coefficient of the curvature contraction determines $C$, as derived in Appendix B.

Time dependence also contributes to the Pontryagin density. The following jet, realizable by a smooth off-shell profile, gives $\mathcal P=-9$ on the sphere.

\stepcounter{equation}
\begin{equation}\tag{D-04}\label{eq:D-04}
q=N=1,\quad \dot q=\ddot q=\dot N=V=\dot V=0,\quad
\eta=\tfrac12,\quad D\eta=1
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad \mathcal P=3-12\chi,\quad \mathcal N=6.
\end{equation}

\subsection{Absolute and relative primitives}

Use the local CS form of the same connection. Let $s_1,s_2,s_3$ be the elementary symmetric polynomials in the diagonal structure eigenvalues. Direct calculation gives the slice primitive

\stepcounter{equation}
\begin{equation}\tag{D-05}\label{eq:D-05}
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{CS}_{\mathcal P}&=-\mathrm{tr}(\omega\wedge d\omega+\tfrac23\omega\wedge\omega\wedge\omega),\\
\int_{\Sigma}\mathrm{CS}_{\mathcal P}&=\mathcal V_{\Sigma}(\Phi_0+\phi),\\
\Phi_0&=s_1^3-4s_1s_2+10s_3,\qquad
s_1=\sum_i n_i,\quad s_2=\sum_{i<j}n_in_j,\quad s_3=n_1n_2n_3,\\
\phi&=-12\chi\eta+4\eta^3-12\eta q^2b^2,\qquad
D\phi=q^3\mathcal P.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}

The constant $\Phi_0$ is the LC reference value in the specified homogeneous frame. It equals $0,-1,0,8$ for $T^3$, Nil, Sol and $S^3$, respectively. It drops out of endpoint differences on a fixed-structure time interval, but is not determined by $\chi$ alone. Absolute CS values in changing local framings are not assigned the meaning of invariant charges.

Choose the LC connection $\bar\omega$ of the same coframe as reference and set $K=\omega-\bar\omega$. With both connections compared in the same frame identification, define the relative form

\stepcounter{equation}
\begin{equation}\tag{D-06}\label{eq:D-06}
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal T_{\mathcal P}&=-2\mathrm{tr}(K\wedge\bar R)-\mathrm{tr}(K\wedge\bar D K)
-\tfrac23\mathrm{tr}(K\wedge K\wedge K),\\
d\mathcal T_{\mathcal P}&=-\mathrm{tr}(R\wedge R)+\mathrm{tr}(\bar R\wedge\bar R),\\
\int_{\Sigma}\mathcal T_{\mathcal P}&=\mathcal V_{\Sigma}\phi,\qquad
\int_M\mathcal P e^{0123}=\mathcal V_{\Sigma}[\phi]_i^f.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}

LC Pontryagin vanishes on this family, so the relative primitive differentiates to $\mathcal P$. Under a simultaneous local Lorentz transformation, $K,\bar R,\bar D K$ transform by conjugation; cyclicity of the trace makes the relative form invariant. This does not compare independently chosen endpoint frames without an identification or assert quantization. The NY integral likewise equals the oriented difference of $Q_{\Sigma}$.

\subsection{Selected quadratic and cubic contractions}

This subsection concerns the common-scale, two-amplitude family. Use $x=Db+Hb$, $\bar y=b^2+(\chi-\eta^2)/q^2$, $z=-D\eta/q$, $w=2b\eta/q$. All indices are raised and lowered with the Lorentz metric; pair-exchange symmetry of EC curvature is not assumed.

\stepcounter{equation}
\begin{equation}\tag{I-01}\label{eq:I-01}
\begin{aligned}
J_2&=\mathrm{tr}(X^2),& J_3&=\mathrm{tr}(X^3),\\
I_1&=R_{abcd}R^{abcd},& I_2&=R_{abcd}R^{cdab},\\
I_{\mathrm{Ric}}&=\mathrm{Ric}_{ab}\mathrm{Ric}^{ab},&
C_{\mathrm{Ric}}&=\mathrm{Ric}^a{}_b\mathrm{Ric}^b{}_c\mathrm{Ric}^c{}_a,\\
C_R&=R_{ab}{}^{cd}R_{cd}{}^{ef}R_{ef}{}^{ab}.&&
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}

$C_R$ sums all ordered indices, without the factors of $1/2$ sometimes used for curvature operators on two-forms. Direct contraction of the curvature blocks gives

\stepcounter{equation}
\begin{equation}\tag{I-02}\label{eq:I-02}
\begin{aligned}
I_1&=12(x^2+\bar y^2-w^2-z^2)+4J_2/q^4,\\
I_2&=12(x^2+\bar y^2+2wz)+4J_2/q^4,\\
I_{\mathrm{Ric}}&=12(x^2+x\bar y+\bar y^2)+J_2/q^4,\\
I_1-I_2&=-12(w+z)^2.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}

\stepcounter{equation}
\begin{equation}\tag{I-03}\label{eq:I-03}
\begin{aligned}
C_{\mathrm{Ric}}&=27x^3+3(x+2\bar y)^3
 +3(x+2\bar y)J_2/q^4+J_3/q^6,\\
C_R&=24[x^3+\bar y^3+3wz(x+\bar y)]
 +24\bar y J_2/q^4-8J_3/q^6.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}

\stepcounter{equation}
\begin{equation}\tag{I-04}\label{eq:I-04}
\left.C_{abcd}C^{abcd}\right|_{\mathrm{LC}}=2J_2/q^4.
\end{equation}

Here $C_{abcd}$ is the LC Weyl tensor; no EC Weyl interpretation is implied. On the linear reduced Nil and Sol trajectories, $x=\bar y=w=z=0$ still leaves $I_{\mathrm{Ric}}=J_2/q^4\ne0$. Vanishing selected reduced scalars therefore does not imply vanishing curvature. Appendix B derives the contractions and trace subtraction.
