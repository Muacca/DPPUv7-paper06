\section{Structure constants and component conventions}

\subsection{Jacobi identity and Bianchi class}

Define the structure constants by $d\sigma^i=\tfrac12C^i{}_{jk}\sigma^j\wedge\sigma^k$ and represent them by five coefficients. Their nonzero scale-stripped entries are listed below, with antisymmetric lower indices. The Jacobi residual and trace give the conditions for this representation to define a Lie algebra and to be unimodular.

\stepcounter{equation}
\begin{equation}\tag{A-01}\label{eq:A-01}
\begin{aligned}
C^1{}_{23}&=a,\quad C^2{}_{31}=b,\quad C^3{}_{12}=c,\quad
C^2{}_{12}=u,\quad C^3{}_{13}=v,\\
J_{123}&=(-a(u+v),0,0),\qquad C^j{}_{ij}=(u+v,0,0).
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}

Thus the Lie family requires $a(u+v)=0$, and the unimodular sector used in the paper has $u+v=0$; it is (G-01) with $n=\mathrm{diag}(a,b,c)$ and $n_{23}=n_{32}=u$. Polynomial identities outside the Jacobi locus are not statements about Lie-group curvature. Sol with $u=1,v=-1$ is class A; an orientation-preserving rotation by $\pi/4$ in the 23-plane diagonalizes its structure matrix. The general formulas in the text refer to this class-A sector.

\subsection{Component convention for complete NY}

With the oriented alternating symbol $[0123]=+1$, the component coefficient in complete NY is $1/4$ on the torsion side and $1/2$ on the curvature side. This symbol is distinct from the raised Lorentz epsilon tensor.

\stepcounter{equation}
\begin{equation}\tag{A-02}\label{eq:A-02}
\mathcal N=\tfrac14[abcd]T^e{}_{ab}T_{ecd}
-\tfrac12[abcd]R_{abcd}.
\end{equation}

\subsection{Periodic rotating coframe on a fixed torus}

Let $x^1,x^2,x^3$ have period $2\pi$ and $\nu\in\mathbb Z$. The following $O(x^3)\in SO(3)$ is global, and $\sigma=O(dx^1,dx^2,dx^3)^T$ is an orthonormal coframe for the same flat metric.

\stepcounter{equation}
\begin{equation}\tag{F-01}\label{eq:F-01}
\begin{aligned}
O(x^3)&=\begin{pmatrix}\cos\nu x^3&\sin\nu x^3&0\\-\sin\nu x^3&\cos\nu x^3&0\\0&0&1\end{pmatrix},\\
d\sigma^1&=-\nu\sigma^2\wedge\sigma^3,\quad
d\sigma^2=-\nu\sigma^3\wedge\sigma^1,\quad d\sigma^3=0,\\
n&=\mathrm{diag}(-\nu,-\nu,0),\qquad
\omega=-dO\,O^T,\quad \omega^1{}_2=-\nu dx^3.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}

Direct calculation gives $d\sigma+\omega\wedge\sigma=0$ and $d\omega+\omega\wedge\omega=0$. Integer periodicity gives $O(x^3+2\pi)=O(x^3)$, so vector parallel transport is the identity around each closed period. For $\nu\ne0$, constant $\alpha_i$ satisfy $d(\alpha_i\sigma^i)=0$ only when $\alpha_1=\alpha_2=0$. In contrast, $dx^1,dx^2,dx^3$ are global closed forms with variable coefficients in this coframe; their period matrix on the three coordinate cycles is $2\pi I$.

The upper bound $b_1=3$ also follows from Fourier decomposition on the flat torus: each nonzero-wavevector component of a closed one-form is parallel to its wavevector and is exact. Only three zero-mode components remain in cohomology. Thus the number of closed forms in the constant-coefficient coframe subspace is not a Betti number. This comparison concerns the specified static metric and vector connection, not spin structures or equivalence of the full dynamical ansatz in both frames.
