\section{Derivation of the curvature identities}

In a diagonal frame, $de^i=H e^{0i}+(n_i/q)e^{jk}$. The spatial coefficients in (G-04) obey the following relation, so the spatial torsion from the first Cartan equation is $2\eta/q$.

\stepcounter{equation}
\begin{equation}\tag{R-01}\label{eq:R-01}
a_j+a_k=\frac{n_i-2\eta}{q},\qquad
\frac{n_i}{q}-a_j-a_k=\frac{2\eta}{q},\qquad H-b=V/3.
\end{equation}

Expanding the second Cartan equation gives temporal boost coefficient $Db+Hb$ and spatial boost coefficient $b(n_i/q-a_j-a_k)$. The rotation coefficients are given below. Cancellation of the spatial term linear in $\eta$ is the key to the common formula.

\stepcounter{equation}
\begin{equation}\tag{R-02}\label{eq:R-02}
\begin{aligned}
Da_i+Ha_i&=-D\eta/q,\\
b^2+\frac{n_i a_i}{q}-a_ja_k
&=b^2+\frac{n_i\ell_i-\ell_j\ell_k-\eta^2}{q^2},\\
\ell_j+\ell_k&=n_i.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}

This proves (D-01). Spatial scalar and Ricci curvature follow by setting torsion, $b$ and $Db$ to zero. Summing the $k_i$ yields (G-02) and the four representatives give the displayed $\chi$ and tracefree Ricci tensors. Every real symmetric $n$ can be brought to this frame by a constant $SO(3)$ rotation, so the scalar identities are not artifacts of a preferred diagonal display.

In the Pontryagin wedge summed over all internal indices, the three boost pairs contribute $-12xw$ and the rotation pairs contribute $4z\sum_i y_i$. The internal-dual contraction weights purely spatial form pairs by $+1$ and temporal form pairs by $-1$, giving $w\sum_i y_i-3xz$. These contractions prove (D-02).

On the static restriction $H=Db=D\eta=0$, $b=-V/3$, one obtains $\mathcal B=3w\bar y$. Extracting the coefficient linear in $V\eta$ from this polynomial yields $C=-9\chi$.

NY is also checked by direct wedge products. The $\eta V$ terms cancel between the two sides, leaving precisely the time derivative of $Q$.

\stepcounter{equation}
\begin{equation}\tag{R-03}\label{eq:R-03}
\begin{aligned}
T^a\wedge T_a&=\frac{4V\eta}{q}e^{0123},\\
e^a\wedge e^b\wedge R_{ab}
&=\left(\frac{4V\eta}{q}-\frac{6(D\eta+2H\eta)}q\right)e^{0123}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}

Expansion of the CS form from the connection gives the following constant part at $\eta=0$. Expressing it in elementary symmetric polynomials yields $\Phi_0$ in (D-05).

\stepcounter{equation}
\begin{equation}\tag{R-04}\label{eq:R-04}
\Phi_0=\sum_i n_i^3-\sum_{i\ne j}n_i^2n_j+4n_1n_2n_3
=s_1^3-4s_1s_2+10s_3.
\end{equation}

Differentiating the remaining $\phi$ and using $Dq=Hq$, $Db=x-Hb$ gives $-12(\chi-\eta^2)D\eta-12q^2b^2D\eta-24q^2b\eta x=q^3\mathcal P$. This is also an independent comparison between the local density and the endpoint primitive.

The general relative form (D-06) follows from the connection path $\omega_\gamma=\bar\omega+\gamma K$, with $R_\gamma=\bar R+\gamma\bar D K+\gamma^2K\wedge K$. Bianchi identity and cyclicity give the formula below; integration from $\gamma=0$ to 1 proves (D-06). This argument does not depend on the four representatives.

\stepcounter{equation}
\begin{equation}\tag{R-05}\label{eq:R-05}
\partial_\gamma[-\mathrm{tr}(R_\gamma\wedge R_\gamma)]
=-2d\,\mathrm{tr}(K\wedge R_\gamma).
\end{equation}

\subsection{Ordered contractions and the LC Weyl square}

If $X_i$ are the eigenvalues of $X$, then $y_i=\bar y-X_i/q^2$ and $\sum_iX_i=0$. Hence $\sum y_i=3\bar y$, $\sum y_i^2=3\bar y^2+J_2/q^4$, and $\sum y_i^3=3\bar y^3+3\bar yJ_2/q^4-J_3/q^6$. Ordered-index contraction of the curvature blocks gives

\stepcounter{equation}
\begin{equation}\tag{I-06}\label{eq:I-06}
\begin{aligned}
I_1&=4\sum_i(x^2+y_i^2-w^2-z^2),\\
I_2&=4\sum_i(x^2+y_i^2+2wz),\\
C_R&=8\sum_i[x^3+y_i^3+3wz(x+y_i)],\\
\mathrm{Ric}^a{}_b&=\mathrm{diag}
 (3x,x+2\bar y+X_1/q^2,x+2\bar y+X_2/q^2,x+2\bar y+X_3/q^2).
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}

The differing negative and cross terms in the first two lines follow from the Lorentz metric and the exchanged index pairs. Squaring and cubing the last diagonal matrix gives the Ricci contractions in (I-02), (I-03). In the LC restriction, $w=z=0$ and $R=6(x+\bar y)$. Substitution into the four-dimensional trace subtraction $C^2=I_1-2I_{\mathrm{Ric}}+R^2/3$ cancels all $x,\bar y$ terms and proves (I-04). These calculations derive the selected contractions without an external code prerequisite.

\subsection{Ricci spectrum and inverse structure matrix}

For real symmetric tracefree $X$, Newton identities give $\sum_{i<j}X_iX_j=-J_2/2$ and $\det X=J_3/3$, proving (I-05). Three real roots require nonnegative discriminant $J_2^3/2-3J_3^2$; conversely this condition with $J_2\ge0$ gives three real roots. The case $J_2=0$ means $X=0$, while zero discriminant means repeated roots. Neither is to be conflated with a zero Ricci eigenvalue below.

At unit scale, let $r_i=X_i+2\chi$ be the spatial Ricci eigenvalues. With $s_1=\mathrm{tr}n$ and $\ell_i=s_1/2-n_i$, one has $n_i=\ell_j+\ell_k$ and $r_i=2\ell_j\ell_k$, directly by substitution into $k_j+k_k$. At physical scale $q$, the eigenvalues are $r_i/q^2$.

\stepcounter{equation}
\begin{equation}\tag{I-07}\label{eq:I-07}
r_i\ne0\ (\forall i):\qquad r_1r_2r_3>0,\qquad \ell_i^2=\frac{r_jr_k}{2r_i},\qquad n_i=\ell_j+\ell_k.
\end{equation}

In the nondegenerate branch, positive product is necessary and also sufficient: the magnitudes in (I-07) and the sign conditions $r_i=2\ell_j\ell_k$ construct the solution. Choosing the sign of $\ell_1$ fixes $\ell_2=r_3/(2\ell_1)$ and $\ell_3=r_2/(2\ell_1)$; the remaining equation follows automatically. Thus only the overall sign and orthogonal eigenspace basis remain. Permutations and conjugacies of real symmetric matrices can be implemented by $SO(3)$ conjugacy.

If two $r_i$ are nonzero, all three $\ell_i$ are nonzero, forcing the third $r_i$ to be nonzero: exactly two is impossible. If $r_1\ne0$ and $r_2=r_3=0$, then $\ell_1=0$, $\ell_2\ell_3=r_1/2$, leaving a continuous product family. For example, $\ell=(0,t,t^{-1})$, $t\ne0$, gives $n=(t+t^{-1},t^{-1},t)$ with the same $r=(2,0,0)$. If all $r_i=0$, at most one $\ell_i$ is nonzero, so $n\sim(a,a,0)$. This is an algebraic inverse problem for structure matrices, not a classification of local isometry or compact quotients.
